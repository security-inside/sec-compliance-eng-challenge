# Indicative compliance mapping

This mapping is a discussion aid, not a control assessment, certification, or claim of compliance. Actual applicability depends on organizational scope, risk assessment, control ownership, and operating evidence.

| Framework | What this prototype can help demonstrate | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISO/IEC 27001:2022 | A small technical mechanism for secure configuration evidence, access restriction, event traceability, and integrity checking. | ISMS scope, risk treatment, control applicability, operating effectiveness, incident handling, supplier governance, or certification. |
| ISO/IEC 27701:2025 | Data minimization discussion, access restrictions, stated retention intent, and awareness that process/session data may identify people. | Lawful basis, controller/processor roles, notices, rights handling, DPIA, deletion execution, or a PIMS. Confirm which edition the organization has adopted. |
| SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria | Technical evidence that a sample service authenticates ingestion, validates inputs, restricts queries, and records operational events. | A SOC 2 report, control-owner evidence, population completeness, period-of-time operating effectiveness, or auditor conclusions. |
| PCI DSS v4.0.1 | An example of secure evidence handling only if the system is in scope or can affect the CDE; avoid sending payment data. | PCI DSS compliance or CDE scope reduction. Applicability must be determined by the entity and its assessor based on connectivity and impact. |
| ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | An explicit scope statement: this prototype has no AI system or AI decision-making function. | AI governance controls. If AI is introduced, inventory, risk assessment, lifecycle governance, monitoring, and accountability require a separate analysis. |

The current ISO catalogue lists ISO/IEC 27701:2025 as the published edition; the source brief references 2019. Confirm the edition applicable to the interview scenario instead of silently asserting equivalence.
